## 2. Review Register

### Item 01: Shandilya Bhakti Sutra Numbering & Cross-Edition Variorum
- **Location**: Chapter 03, Section 1 (`शांडिल्य भक्ती सूत्र ३६ : स च भक्तिप्रसादात्॥`)
- **Nature of Item**: Textual recension variation in source numbering.
- **Editorial Note**:
  - In certain classical commentaries (e.g., Svapneshvara Bhashya), this sutra appears as Sutra 36 (*sa ca bhaktiprasādāt*).
  - In alternative North Indian editions (e.g., Acharya Goswami edition), it is grouped in the second Ahika as Sutra 37.
  - **Current Treatment in Manuscript**: Retained approved canonical numbering `#36` as verified in the project's source database (`data/raw/shandilya_bhakti_sutra.json`).
  - **Action Required from Author**: Confirm whether `#36` or an explicit edition reference (e.g., *Svapneshvara recension*) is preferred in the printed apparatus.

### Item 02: Transliteration Representation of Avagraha (ऽ)
- **Location**: Chapter 04, Section 5 (`पतंजली योगसूत्र १.१४ : स तु दीर्घकालनैरन्तर्यसत्काराऽऽसेवितो दृढभूमिः ॥`) and Chapter 10, Section 1 (`नारद भक्ती सूत्र १० : अन्याश्रयाणां त्यागोऽनन्यता॥`)
- **Nature of Item**: Orthographic styling in IAST transliteration.
- **Editorial Note**:
  - In Devanagari, the avagraha (`ऽ`) represents the elision of a vowel resulting from Sandhi.
  - In standard academic IAST, some presses render this with an apostrophe (e.g., *tyāgo'nanyatā*), while general reader editions occasionally omit the apostrophe for simplicity (*tyāgo nanyatā* or *tyāgonanyatā*).
  - **Current Treatment in Manuscript**: Standard academic apostrophe retained (*tyāgo'nanyatā* and *satkārā'āsevito*).
  - **Action Required from Author**: Confirm whether to retain apostrophes or use simplified popular phonetic transliteration for general readers.

### Item 03: Biographical Case Study Diversity Across Chapters
- **Location**: Chapters 01 through 10, Section 5
- **Nature of Item**: Case study distribution.
- **Editorial Note**:
  - Jamsetji Tata is referenced in all 10 chapters as the paramount Indian exemplar of ethical nation-building, social stewardship, and visionary persistence.
  - Western exemplars are appropriately varied across chapters: Thomas Edison (experimentation/perseverance in Ch 1, 2, 3, 6), Andrew Carnegie (charitable wealth in Ch 4), Milton Hershey (children's school trust in Ch 5, 7, 8, 10), and James Burke (Tylenol recall crisis management in Ch 9).
  - **Current Treatment in Manuscript**: Retained all approved canonical case studies verbatim without inventing or removing figures.
  - **Action Required from Author**: In future expanded volumes, author may consider incorporating additional Indian business exemplars (e.g., Walchand Hirachand, V. O. Chidambaram Pillai, Verghese Kurien, or Kirloskar Brothers) to further broaden the Eastern portfolio.

### Item 04: Distinction Between Literal Translation and Interpretive Bhashya
- **Location**: All 10 Chapters, Section 3 vs. Section 4
- **Nature of Item**: Reader clarity between direct semantic meaning and expanded managerial commentary.
- **Editorial Note**:
  - To ensure that readers never mistake contemporary managerial application for the literal grammatical meaning of the Sanskrit words, Section 3 has been strictly restricted to:
    1. Literal Padaccheda & Anvaya.
    2. Direct semantic translation (Marathi & English).
    3. Etymological Nirukta.
  - All extended business metaphors, stakeholder capitalism concepts, and corporate leadership models have been placed exclusively into Section 4 (*सविस्तर विवेचन व निरूपण*) and Section 5 (*व्यावहारिक उपयोजन*).
  - **Current Treatment in Manuscript**: Clean separation maintained throughout.
  - **Action Required from Author**: Verify that this clear segregation aligns with the author's vision.

### Item 05: Presentation of Modern Corporate Failures
- **Location**: Section 6 across chapters
- **Nature of Item**: Compliance with Section 5.E of editorial guidelines regarding failed businesses.
- **Editorial Note**:
  - The editorial guidelines strictly stipulate: *"For business failures, famous failed brands/businesses may be named if relevant and supported; do not name founders, owners, CEOs, directors or other individual people. Anonymise lesser-known businesses. Focus on decisions, consequences and lessons, not personal blame."*
  - In Section 6, the **काय टाळावे (DON'Ts)** subsections focus purely on decisions, behavioral temptations, and structural risks (e.g., valuation inflation, regulatory shortcuts, lack of succession planning) rather than personal naming of failed executives.
  - **Current Treatment in Manuscript**: Full compliance achieved with zero personal blame or libelous references.
